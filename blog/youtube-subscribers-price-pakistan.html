Droppable — the budget option

The Droppable tier starts fast and allows larger orders (up to 10,000), but some subscribers may drop over time and there is no refill. It suits channels that want a quick, low-cost boost to their count.

No Drop — built for stability

The No Drop tier delivers gradually (around 50+ per day, up to 500 per order), which looks natural, and the subscribers are non-drop with a lifetime refill if the count ever falls. It costs more, but suits channels building toward long-term goals like the 1,000-subscriber mark.

What Affects the Price of YouTube Subscribers?

1. Stability & Refills

Subscribers that stay — backed by non-drop delivery and refill guarantees — cost more to source than fast, unguaranteed ones.

2. Delivery Speed

Gradual delivery (tens per day) is safer and looks organic, but slower to fulfil. Instant bulk delivery is cheaper and riskier-looking.

3. Order Size Limits

Stable tiers cap orders (500 per order on No Drop) to keep delivery natural, which raises the per-1,000 price compared with bulk tiers.

What Subscribers Can — and Cannot — Do for Your Channel

Be clear about this before you spend money: subscribers alone do not monetize a channel. The YouTube Partner Program requires 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million valid Shorts views in 90 days — and watch hours must come from genuine viewing of your content. Purchased subscribers help your count and social proof; they do not replace real videos and real watch time. YouTube's fake-engagement policy also prohibits artificial inflation of metrics, so any purchase carries some risk — gradual delivery and sensible quantities reduce it, but no honest service can promise zero risk. The channels that succeed treat a subscriber boost as a head start, then grow with consistent, watchable content.
